Research

Most models are trained on outcomes: a reward at the end of a rollout, a single similarity score, one view of the data. Outcomes say whether a model succeeded, but not where its reasoning went wrong or what it failed to see. I want to give models richer signals to learn from, and my work so far follows three threads.

01

Reinforcement Learning & Post-Training

Language models now learn from their own rollouts, but an outcome reward only says whether the final answer was right. I study denser on-policy signals: distilling a solution-aware teacher's attention so the student learns where to look, and reasons more accurately with far shorter rollouts.

02

Multimodal Learning

Each modality sees part of the picture. I fuse complementary views (appearance and skeleton for sign language; time, frequency and text for forecasting; visible context and language priors for occluded objects) into representations that hold up where any single view fails.

03

Embodied AI

Where the first two threads meet. A vision–language model watches a robot's failed episode, diagnoses why it failed, and grounds that feedback in the decisive moments. The diagnosis then becomes reward shaping for reinforcement learning, fading as the policy becomes competent.
