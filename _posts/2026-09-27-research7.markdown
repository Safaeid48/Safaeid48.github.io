<h2>Abstract</h2>
<p class="abstract">On-policy self-distillation trains reasoning models on their own trajectories using dense token distribution guidance from a privileged teacher with access to a verified solution. This supervision transfers what the teacher predicts without directly transferring where it attends within the preceding context. We introduce On-Policy Attention Self-Distillation (OPASD), which complements token-level supervision with solution-conditioned attention distillation. Because the privileged teacher can attend to verified solution tokens unavailable to the student, OPASD projects teacher attention onto student-visible positions and renormalizes the resulting distribution before alignment. Across three model sizes and four competition-level mathematics benchmarks, OPASD consistently outperforms token-only OPSD, improving average accuracy by 4.98 to 8.40 percentage points. OPASD also avoids the response-length inflation and performance degradation observed with token-only distillation, reducing generated rollout tokens by 73.9% and estimated model compute by 72.6% while training 1.53× faster. These results show that solution-conditioned attention provides a complementary supervision signal that makes on-policy self-distillation more accurate, stable, and compute-efficient.</p>

<h2>The missing signal</h2>
<p class="kicker">Motivation</p>
<p>In on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), the same model plays two roles. As a <em>student</em> it sees only the problem and its own partial answer; as a <em>privileged teacher</em> it also sees a verified solution. The teacher's next-token distribution is dense, per-token feedback on the student's own trajectory.</p>
<p>But next-token distributions say <em>what</em> to predict, not <em>where</em> to look. In multi-step reasoning, later steps hinge on constraints and intermediate results established much earlier, and the teacher's attention over that context is a solution-conditioned signal that OPSD leaves unused. Distilling it is not trivial: teacher and student attend over different contexts, because only the teacher can attend to solution tokens.</p>

<h2>Method</h2>
<p class="kicker">Four steps per training iteration</p>

<figure class="figure figure--wide">
  <div class="figure__frame"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/papers/opasd/method.webp" alt="OPASD method overview: on-policy sampling, teacher-student distributions, student-support projection, and joint distillation" loading="lazy"></div>
  <figcaption><b>OPASD overview.</b> The student samples a rollout; teacher and student produce token and attention distributions over the same trajectory; the teacher's attention is projected onto student-visible positions and renormalized; and both signals are distilled jointly.</figcaption>
</figure>

<ol class="steps">
  <li><strong>On-policy sampling</strong>The student generates its own reasoning trajectory for a problem <i>x</i>, so supervision is always about states the student actually visits.</li>
  <li><strong>Two views, one trajectory</strong>The teacher re-reads the same prefix with the verified solution <i>y*</i> in context. Both models emit next-token and final-layer attention distributions.</li>
  <li><strong>Student-support projection</strong>Attention mass on solution-only positions is removed and the remainder renormalized, giving a target defined entirely over context the student can see.</li>
  <li><strong>Joint distillation</strong>The student minimizes <i>L</i> = <i>L</i><sub>tok</sub> + λ<sub>attn</sub> <i>L</i><sub>attn</sub>, using JSD for attention, so it learns what to predict and where to look.</li>
</ol>

<h3>More accurate <em>and</em> cheaper</h3>
<p>The extra attention objective does not add cost; it removes it. Because OPASD avoids response-length inflation, rollouts are far shorter, so the whole run is faster and lighter.</p>

<h2>What matters</h2>
<p class="kicker">Ablations on Qwen3-1.7B · average Avg@12</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Divergence:</strong> JSD (32.85) &gt; reverse KL (32.36) &gt; forward KL (31.73). A symmetric, bounded objective balances mode-covering and mode-seeking behaviour.</li>
  <li><strong>Attention weight:</strong> λ<sub>attn</sub> = 0.5 is best (34.02), ahead of 1.0 (32.85) and 2.0 (31.82).</li>
  <li><strong>Depth:</strong> distilling only the final layer (34.02) beats the final two (32.54) or three (31.04) layers.</li>
</ul>
